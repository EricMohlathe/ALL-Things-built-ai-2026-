AScope

What is being tested, and what would count as passing

Every one of these six sources is describing the same structure: mark a price range formed during a defined clock window, wait for price to leave it, trade the leaving. The disagreement is only over which window, which confirmation, and which target.

That shared structure is what makes the family testable as one thing. The 9:30 sources mark the first five minutes of the New York equity open. The ICT source marks 19:00–00:00 New York. The Goldmine source marks 22:00–00:00 GMT. All three then wait for a break, a sweep, or a retest, and enter on it. Same machine, different clocks.

The assertion

Trading this structure produces a positive expected return after spread, slippage and commission, on data the rules were not fitted to. Nothing weaker is worth testing. A strategy that is profitable before costs is not profitable. A strategy that is profitable on the period it was tuned on has not been tested at all.

Materiality

An edge is material only if it exceeds the round-trip cost of harvesting it, with margin. Throughout this file, cost is expressed in R — multiples of the money risked on the trade — because that makes it directly comparable to the edge. On a gold trade risking 300 points with a 25-point spread, one round trip plus modest slippage costs roughly 0.2 R. An edge of 0.04 R per trade does not survive that. This is the single arithmetic fact that kills most retail systems, and almost no source in this file mentions it.

Scope limitations, stated up front

  • Three of the six sources are marketing artifacts for paid products. They are treated as claims to be tested, not as evidence.
  • No peer-reviewed test of the ICT sweep-and-shift model exists. A search of the literature returns vendor content and platform blogs only. That absence is a finding, not a gap in this review.
  • The largest available test of the 9:30 rules was run on US equities, not FX or gold. Its conclusions transfer as a strong prior, not as proof.
  • This file cannot test what a discretionary trader sees on a live tape. It can only test the teachable, mechanical version — which is the version being sold.

B-3 · The unified state machine

Everything above collapses into one machine with a selectable entry model. This is what the accompanying Expert Advisor implements, and writing it out this way is what makes the family falsifiable.

0
IDLE
Outside the range window. Clock converted from broker time to New York time with the US daylight-saving rule applied, because every source anchors to New York and none of them mention that this shifts twice a year.
1
RANGE_FORMING
Accumulate high and low across the window. For a window that crosses midnight, the range belongs to the following trade day.
2
RANGE_SET
Range fixed. Structural filters applied: minimum width, maximum width as a multiple of ATR. A range far wider than normal volatility is not a range, it is a trend already in progress.
3
ARMED
Inside the entry window, waiting for the selected model to fire. Directional bias evaluated here, not at entry, so it cannot be quietly relaxed when a trade looks good.
4a
BREAK_DIRECT
First body close beyond the boundary. No retest, no confirmation candle. This is the variant the evidence supports — see C-2.
4b
BREAK_FVG
Break plus a three-candle fair value gap in the displacement leg. The TradingView script's BREAK entry.
4c
TRAP
Break out, close back inside, close back outside again in the original direction. The script's TRAP entry.
4d
RETEST
Break, pullback to the broken level, rejection close. The version taught in every video. Implemented so it can be measured against the others, not because it is recommended.
4e
SWEEP_MSS
Wick through the boundary with a close back inside, then a body close through the last confirmed fractal swing in the opposite direction. The ICT model, made mechanical.
5
SIZED
Structural stop clamped between an ATR floor and ceiling, position sized from account equity, spread checked against both an absolute cap and a percentage of the stop. A 25-point spread against a 40-point stop is not a trade at any win rate.
6
MANAGED
Peak favourable and adverse excursion recorded tick by tick. Optional breakeven, partial, ATR trail. Forced flat at session end.
7
JOURNALLED
Row written with realised R, excursions in R, entry spread, range width relative to ATR, and the session date — the last of which is the field that makes honest statistics possible.
CTests of detail

What the evidence says

C-1 · The direct test of the taught rules

One of the uploaded sources is a mechanical backtest of exactly the rules in B-1: 165,336 simulated trades across 359 US stocks, 2020 to mid-2026, spanning a bear market, a crash, two calm bull stretches, a choppy period and a volatility spike. It is by a wide margin the most useful document in the file, and its findings are unflattering.

Findings — 165,336 mechanical trades on the taught rule set

Read the last column of that table as an audit finding rather than a statistic. The win rate does not merely fail to beat the payout-implied break-even — it tracks it, at every one of thirteen target sizes tested from 1:1 to 10:1. That tracking is the signature of a process carrying no directional information. The payout sets the win rate; the entry contributes nothing.

Finding C-1.1 — the confirmation rule is the most expensive rule in the system

Every video source insists on waiting for a decisive rejection candle at the retest. Tested, that rule moved the win rate down, from 33.0% to 31.9% on untouched holdout data, significant at p < 0.001. The mechanism is not mysterious: a large obvious rejection candle means the move already happened. You enter later, at a worse price, and the stop is wider because it is placed beyond a bigger candle. The rule sold as the thing that separates winners from losers is measurably the thing making results worse. Tick: ✗

Finding C-1.2 — the excursion symmetry

After entry, the average trade travelled about as far in the trader's favour (1.29 R) as against them (1.23 R). A signal with directional content produces lopsided excursions. These are not lopsided. The accompanying forensics tool measures this on your own data, with the correction described in F-2. Tick: ✗

Finding C-1.3 — the eleven Mondays

The same author searched for any pocket where the strategy worked and found one: long trades, Mondays, bull markets. It survived a fresh data slice. Then he counted the underlying sessions — the hundreds of "Monday trades" came from eleven distinct Mondays, and two of those eleven produced 92% of the apparent edge. Counted correctly, the result vanished. Keep this in view for E-2: it is the exact failure mode that makes bad backtests look rigorous.

C-2 · The version that does work, and what it deletes

Against that, there is a peer-reviewed opening-range study with the opposite conclusion: Zarattini, Barbon and Aziz, A Profitable Day Trading Strategy For The U.S. Equity Market (Swiss Finance Institute Research Paper 24-98, February 2024), covering more than 7,000 US stocks from 2016 to 2023. It finds the 5-minute opening range breakout profitable, with a large improvement from restricting trades to stocks in play — names with abnormal volume driven by company news — and it holds after transaction costs. A public QuantConnect replication reports a Sharpe near 2.4 with beta close to zero.

The interesting part is what that strategy does not contain.

Finding C-2.1 — the edge, where there is one, lives in selection and exit, not entry

The backtest article independently reproduced this. Filtering to the top quartile by pre-market relative volume lifted the win rate from 38.2% to 39.9% and average return from +0.031 R to +0.041 R per trade, significant at p = 0.004. That is a genuine, measurable selection effect. It is also smaller than a five-basis-point round trip, which erased it entirely. So: a real effect, correctly identified, that still did not clear the cost floor at that implementation. Tick: ~

The practical instruction from C-1 and C-2 together is unusually crisp. Every rule that sources add to improve the entry — retest, confirmation, structure shift — subtracts. Every element that survives testing sits either before the entry (what you trade, and when) or after it (how long you hold, and what it costs). Direct your effort accordingly.

C-3 · What actually happens at a swept boundary

No peer-reviewed test of the ICT sweep model exists. But the mechanism it describes has been documented, and the documentation is more interesting than either source in this file realises.

Carol Osler's work at the New York Fed, published in the Journal of Finance in 2003, examined the complete order book of a major FX dealing bank — 9,655 orders worth over $55 billion. She found that price-contingent orders cluster, and crucially that the two kinds cluster in different places. Take-profit orders bunch at round numbers; stop-loss orders bunch just beyond them. Her follow-up work on price cascades showed exchange rates move unusually fast after reaching levels where stop-losses cluster.

Map that onto the disagreement in B-2 and it resolves cleanly:

  • Take-profit clustering produces reversals. Resting profit-taking absorbs the move and turns it. This is the ICT sweep — the trap, the reversal — and it is a real documented mechanism.
  • Stop-loss clustering produces continuations. Triggered stops become market orders in the direction of the break, which triggers more of them. This is the Goldmine breakout — also real, also documented.
  • Both order types sit around the same boundary. Which cluster dominates on any given touch is not something the touch itself tells you.
Finding C-3.1 — the sweep is a volatility signal, not a direction signal

Crossing a session boundary reliably predicts that something will happen. It does not predict which way. That is precisely why two authors can build opposite systems on the same level and both show winning screenshots. Any workable version of this needs a directional input from somewhere other than the sweep — which is, to be fair to the ICT source, exactly what its insistence on a higher-timeframe bias is trying to supply. Tick: ~ on the mechanism, ⌀ on the trading edge.

C-4 · The base rate you are trading against

Before any of this, there is a prior. Chague, De-Losso and Giovannetti tracked every individual who began day trading Brazilian equity futures between 2013 and 2015. Of those who persisted beyond 300 sessions, 97% lost money; 1.1% earned more than the Brazilian minimum wage; 0.5% earned more than a bank teller's starting salary. Barber, Lee, Liu and Odean, using complete Taiwan Stock Exchange records from 1992 to 2006, found fewer than 1% predictably profitable net of fees. Both studies found no evidence of learning over time.

This does not prove that no strategy works. It sets the prior odds that any given mechanical intraday system, arrived at by reading six articles, is one of them. Update accordingly, and note that both papers used complete exchange records — not surveys, not self-reports, not screenshots.

A note on the ICT source specifically

The ICT Asian range document is a different animal from the Goldmine ones. It sells an ebook, but the strategy content is specific, internally consistent, honest about failure modes — it explicitly says price does not sweep the range every day and that you should sit out — and it lists its own common mistakes. That is materially better epistemic conduct than the others. It still supplies no evidence of profitability, and no such evidence exists in the literature. Good faith is not a substitute for a track record. Tick: ⌀

The one addition not in any source

Cost, as a first-class parameter. The restated model refuses any entry where the spread exceeds a set percentage of the stop distance, and the journal records the spread on every fill so the forensics tool can find the exact cost level at which the edge dies. If that number is close to what you actually pay, you do not have a strategy — you have a rebate scheme running in the wrong direction.

EEvidence standard

The standard a backtest has to meet before it means anything

These are the gates the accompanying forensics tool enforces. They are not optional rigour. Each one corresponds to a specific way the sources in this file went wrong.

E-1 · Sessions, not trades

Two trades on the same morning share the same news, the same regime, the same range. They are one observation, not two. Sample size is the number of distinct sessions. A thousand trades over sixty days is sixty pieces of evidence. Every confidence interval in the forensics tool is computed by resampling whole sessions.

E-2 · Concentration

Compute the share of gross profit produced by the best two sessions. If it exceeds half, you have two lucky days wearing a strategy costume. This is the eleven-Mondays test from C-1.3, generalised.

E-3 · Cost sweep, not cost assumption

Do not test at one cost level. Sweep the round-trip cost upward until expectancy crosses zero and report that number. Then compare it to what you actually pay. Margin, not sign, is the answer.

E-4 · Held-out data, decided in advance

Split by session, chronologically, before you look. The held-out slice is spent once. If you tune after seeing it, it is no longer held out and you have to find a new period.

E-5 · Pay for what you searched

Every parameter set, symbol, timeframe and entry model you tried is a lottery ticket. Testing twelve variants at the 5% level means you should expect roughly one to look significant by chance. The forensics tool applies a Šidák correction: at twelve variants, the bar moves from p < 0.05 to p < 0.0043. Count your variants honestly, including the ones you abandoned.

A failed gate is not a prompt to keep tuning. Tuning until the gate passes is the mechanism by which a coin flip becomes a backtest.

E-6 · Kill criteria, written before the test

Decide now what result makes you stop. Suggested: fewer than 100 sessions, or a lower confidence bound at or below zero, or an edge that dies below your real cost, or a failed out-of-sample slice. Write it down before you run anything, because the version of you looking at a promising equity curve is not the person you want setting this threshold.

FImplementation

Running it

F-1 · The Expert Advisor

GODMODE_SessionRange_Forge.mq5 implements the full state machine from B-3 with all five entry models selectable, so the same code produces the taught version and the restated version and the difference between them is one dropdown. That is deliberate: the comparison is the experiment.

Notable implementation details, since they are where this kind of code usually breaks:

  • Clock. Broker time is converted to New York time with the actual US daylight-saving rule — second Sunday of March, first Sunday of November. Sessions anchored to New York shift against a fixed-offset broker twice a year, and a backtest that ignores this silently trades the wrong window for several months of every year.
  • Midnight-crossing ranges. The Asian preset's 19:00–00:00 window belongs to the following trade day. Handled explicitly.
  • No lookahead. Every signal reads closed bars only. New-bar gating on both the range and entry timeframes.
  • Rounding down. Position sizing floors to the lot step and refuses the trade if the floor is below the broker minimum, rather than rounding up and quietly over-risking.
  • Optimiser guard. OnTester returns zero below 100 trades and scales by the square root of trade count, so the optimiser cannot rank a nine-trade fluke at the top.

F-2 · The forensics engine

srf_forensics.py reads the journal and runs the E-series gates. It has a --demo mode that generates a synthetic 2:1 coin flip with realistic session clustering; run that first, because watching the tool correctly refuse to certify a known-worthless process is how you learn to trust it on a real one. 

python3 srf_forensics.py --demo --variants 12 --plots demo.png
python3 srf_forensics.py journal.csv --target-r 2.0 --variants 12 --plots out.png
One correction this tool makes that the source article does not

Comparing raw mean favourable excursion to raw mean adverse excursion is confounded: a winner's favourable excursion is truncated at the take profit and a loser's adverse excursion is truncated at the stop, so the exit rule manufactures part of the asymmetry. The tool therefore reports the two quantities that are free to vary — favourable excursion given a loss, and adverse excursion given a win — each as a fraction of its own ceiling. When both sit near half their ceiling, the path is wandering between two barriers, which is what a directionless entry looks like. It is reported as a diagnostic rather than a pass/fail gate, because the clean version of the test is directionally informative but not sharp enough to hang a verdict on.

F-3 · Order of operations

  1. Run --demo. Read the output until the gates make sense.
  2. Backtest MODEL_BREAK_DIRECT on one instrument, two years, real spread. One instrument. Resist the sweep.
  3. Run the forensics with --variants 1. Record every gate.
  4. Now run MODEL_RETEST on identical settings. You are testing finding C-1.1 on your own market. If the retest version is worse, you have replicated the central result and you can stop believing the videos on your own evidence rather than on mine.
  5. Only then vary anything else — and increment --variants every single time you do.
  6. Forward-test on demo for a minimum of forty sessions before any live capital. The forward test is the only sample nobody tuned on.
GOpinion

Opinion

Adverse opinion on the strategy as taught

In my judgement, the session-range breakout strategy as specified across these six sources — mark the range, wait for the break, wait for the retest, enter on a confirmation candle, target a fixed 2:1 — does not support the assertion of positive expectancy net of costs. Direct mechanical testing across 165,336 trades finds a win rate tracking the payout-implied break-even at every target size, and the confirmation rule promoted most heavily by the sources measurably reduces performance.

Basis for the adverse opinion

  • The tested win rate sits at or below break-even before costs, in every configuration, including every filter the sources recommend.
  • Excursions after entry are close to symmetric, indicating the entry carries no directional information.
  • The one filter that produced a real, statistically significant improvement did not survive a five-basis-point round trip.
  • Two sources build opposite systems on the same level, and the microstructure evidence explains why both can show winning examples without either having an edge.
  • The commercial sources make win-rate claims that fail an elementary expectancy check by two to three orders of magnitude.

Qualified opinion on the restated model

The restated model in section D — break-direct entry, mechanical direction, volume-based selection, time-based exit, explicit cost gate — is consistent with the published research and with the parts of the backtest evidence that survived. I express no opinion on whether it is profitable on your instrument, at your costs, in the current regime. That is a question your own out-of-sample test answers, and section E specifies what would count as an answer.

Emphasis of matter

Nothing here is financial advice, and I am not a financial adviser. Two studies using complete exchange records found that 97% of persistent retail day traders lost money and fewer than 1% were reliably profitable after fees. That is the population you would be joining. The best outcome available from this file may well be the tooling and the evidence standard, applied to something else — the forensics gates in section E are the transferable asset here, and they work on any strategy, including the next one somebody sells you.
