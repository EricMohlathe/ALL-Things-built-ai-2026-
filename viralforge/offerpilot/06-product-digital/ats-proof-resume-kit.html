Start hereHow the ATS rejects you (and how to win)

An Applicant Tracking System scans your CV before a human does. It checks for keywords from the job post and whether it can even read your layout. Beat those two things and you're in the human pile.

The 4 reasons you're getting filtered:
  1. Missing keywords — your CV doesn't contain the exact terms in the job post.
  2. Unreadable layout — columns, tables, text boxes and graphics confuse the parser.
  3. Wrong section names — creative headings ("My Journey") get skipped.
  4. No measurable wins — duties without numbers don't stand out to humans either.

Part 1The ATS checklist (run this before every submit)

  • ✅ One column. No tables, text boxes, headers/footers, or graphics.
  • ✅ Standard section names: Summary · Experience · Education · Skills.
  • ✅ Save/submit as .docx unless the post asks for PDF.
  • ✅ Mirror 8–12 keywords from the job post (honestly — only what's true).
  • ✅ Every bullet starts with a strong verb and includes a number where possible.
  • ✅ Standard fonts (Calibri, Arial, Inter). No icons for contact details.
  • ✅ Spell out then bracket acronyms: "Search Engine Optimisation (SEO)".
  • ✅ File name: Firstname-Lastname-CV.docx.

Part 5Cover letter + LinkedIn formulas

Cover letter (4 sentences):
1) "I'm applying for [role] at [company] — [one line on why them, specifically]."
2) "In my last role I [biggest relevant achievement with a number]."
3) "I'd bring [skill 1] and [skill 2] to help you [their goal]."
4) "I'd love to show you how — open to a quick call this week?"
LinkedIn headline: "[Role] | I help [who] achieve [outcome] with [skill] | [tool/industry]"
LinkedIn About (opening line): "I help [audience] [get result] — here's how:" then 3 short proof bullets and a CTA to connect.
