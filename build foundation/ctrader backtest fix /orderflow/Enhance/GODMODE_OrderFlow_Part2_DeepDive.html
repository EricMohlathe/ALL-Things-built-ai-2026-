Order Flow & Microstructure

Aggressive Order
A market order that crosses the spread and takes liquidity immediately. The buyer pays the ask, the seller takes the bid. Creates price movement because it consumes available limit orders.
ORDER FLOW
Absorption
When large passive (limit) orders repeatedly absorb aggressive flow WITHOUT moving price. A bullish signal when bids absorb heavy sell pressure — institutions silently accumulating. Visible on footprint charts.
ORDER FLOW
Ask (Offer)
The lowest price at which a seller is willing to sell. Represents sell-side liquidity. Aggressive buyers "hit the ask" when they want to enter immediately.
MICROSTRUCTURE
Bid
The highest price at which a buyer is willing to buy. Represents buy-side liquidity. Aggressive sellers "hit the bid" when they want to enter immediately.
MICROSTRUCTURE
Bid-Ask Spread
The gap between the best bid and best ask price. Tighter spread = more liquid market. Wider spread = less liquid, higher transaction cost. Institutional traders prefer tight-spread instruments.
MICROSTRUCTURE
Cluster Chart
Footprint chart variant (used in ATAS) that shows buy/sell volume at each price within each candle. Reveals exact price levels where aggressive buying or selling occurred.
TOOLS
CVD (Cumulative Volume Delta)
Running total of (aggressive buys minus aggressive sells) over a period. Rising CVD = net buying pressure. Falling CVD = net selling. Most powerful when it diverges from price.
INDICATORS
Delta
The difference between aggressive buying volume and aggressive selling volume within a single candle or period. Positive delta = more buy aggression. Negative delta = more sell aggression.
ORDER FLOW
DOM (Depth of Market)
Also called Level 2. Shows all resting limit orders across multiple price levels on both bid and ask sides. The "order book" — what Bookmap visualizes as a heatmap over time.
TOOLS
Footprint Chart
Advanced chart displaying buy/sell volume at each price tick within each candle bar. Replaces traditional candles with a matrix of bid/ask volume, revealing the internal order flow structure of every bar.
TOOLS
Iceberg Order
A large limit order where only a small portion is visible in the DOM. When consumed, it "refreshes" automatically. Institutions use icebergs to hide order size and prevent front-running. Identifiable via Bookmap bubbles.
INSTITUTIONAL
Imbalance (OFT)
On a footprint chart: a candle row where one side (ask or bid) has significantly more volume than the other. Stacked imbalances = strong directional conviction. Price tends to return to fill imbalances.
ORDER FLOW
MBO (Market By Order)
The most granular level of market data — shows each individual order in the book, not just aggregated levels. CME provides MBO data for futures. Enables precise identification of large player orders vs retail order flow.
DATA
Passive Order
A limit order resting in the order book waiting to be filled. Provides liquidity to the market. When passive orders are consumed, price moves. Institutions often use passive orders to accumulate without moving price.
ORDER FLOW
Spoofing
Placing large limit orders with no intention to fill them — to create the appearance of supply/demand and move price in a desired direction. Illegal in regulated markets but common in crypto. Detectable via Bookmap when large orders disappear before being hit.
MANIPULATION
Unfinished Auction
On a footprint chart: a candle's high printed zero ask volume (buyers ran out, sellers took over) or low printed zero bid volume (sellers ran out, buyers took over). Price will almost always return to "complete" the auction.
FOOTPRINT

Volume Profile

b-Shape Profile
Heavy volume at the bottom of the profile range. Bearish structure — selling dominated that period's auction. Indicates price distribution at lows. Often precedes further downside after completion.
VP SHAPES
Composite Profile
Volume Profile built across multiple sessions (e.g., weekly or monthly). Reveals macro institutional positioning zones. POC on composite = most important price in the entire period — strong S/R.
VP TYPES
D-Shape Profile
Bell-curve profile with volume concentrated in the middle — perfect market balance. Indicates a fair/balanced auction. Price will often range until a catalyst breaks the equilibrium. Common in low-volatility sessions.
VP SHAPES
HVN (High Volume Node)
Price level with abnormally high traded volume. Acts as price magnet — price gravitates toward HVNs during consolidation. Also acts as resistance during fast trend moves. Stall zones and frequent reversal areas.
VP LEVELS
LVN (Low Volume Node)
Price level with minimal volume traded. Price moves through LVNs rapidly — "thin air." Use LVNs as profit targets or acceleration zones. An unfilled LVN is a high-probability return target.
VP LEVELS
P-Shape Profile
Heavy volume concentrated at the top of the range. Bullish structure — buying dominated. Distribution at highs. Often precedes continued upside or consolidation before new leg higher.
VP SHAPES
POC (Point of Control)
The price level with the highest traded volume in the profile. Considered the "fairest price" — where the market spent the most time. Acts as powerful S/R. Price gravitates toward and revolves around the POC.
VP LEVELS
Poor High / Poor Low
A profile extreme (high or low) formed by a single print with very little volume — price moved quickly to that level and reversed. Not a "tested" level. Price will almost always return to retest poor highs/lows properly.
VP LEVELS
Single Print
A TPO (time-price opportunity) level that only occurred in one time bracket — a gap in the profile. Same as a naked LVN or "ledge." Market will always return to fill single prints. Trade target on all timeframes.
VP / MARKET PROFILE
VAH (Value Area High)
The upper boundary of the Value Area — the price level above which only 15% of volume was traded. Resistance in uptrends. Acceptance above VAH = bullish. Rejection at VAH = potential reversal or range-top.
VP LEVELS
VAL (Value Area Low)
The lower boundary of the Value Area — 15% of volume traded below it. Support in downtrends. Rejection at VAL = bullish. Acceptance below VAL = bearish. Mirror of VAH on the downside.
VP LEVELS
Value Area (VA)
The price range containing 70% of all traded volume in a session. Price inside VA = balanced/fair. Price outside VA = potential imbalance and return to value. The 70% VA rule is the statistical backbone of VP trading.

Smart Money Concepts & ICT

AMD Model (PO3)
Accumulation → Manipulation → Distribution. The 3-phase institutional cycle. Accumulation: quiet limit order buying/selling. Manipulation: false breakout to take stops. Distribution: real directional move begins. The "PO3" — Power of Three.
ICT MODEL
Bearish OB
The last bullish (green) candle before a significant bearish Break of Structure. When price returns to this candle from below, it acts as supply/resistance. The institutional selling origin point.
ORDER BLOCKS
Breaker Block
A failed Order Block that gets violated — it then flips to the opposing direction. A bearish OB that got broken to the upside = now a bullish breaker. One of the highest-probability POIs in ICT methodology.
ORDER BLOCKS
Bullish OB
The last bearish (red) candle before a significant bullish Break of Structure. When price returns to this candle from above, it acts as demand/support. The institutional buying origin point. Enter longs here with order flow confirmation.
ORDER BLOCKS
BSL (Buy-Side Liquidity)
Resting buy stop orders above swing highs and equal highs. Retail longs place stops here; bearish traders place stop-losses here. Institutions drive price up to sweep BSL, collect the liquidity, then reverse down.
LIQUIDITY
CHOCH (Change of Character)
In an uptrend: price breaks below the most recent Higher Low. First signal that buying structure may be failing. Not a confirmed reversal — requires follow-through with Lower High formation. Early warning signal.
STRUCTURE
Daily Bias
The directional outlook for the current trading day determined by HTF structure, PDRA levels, and previous session ranges. Must be established before the session begins. Only take trades aligned with daily bias.
ICT PROCESS
Discount Zone
The lower 50% of any price range (from swing low to swing high). In a bullish trend, institutions buy in the discount zone. OTE (Optimal Trade Entry) is in the discount zone after a BSL sweep — 62-79% Fibonacci retracement.
PRICE LEVELS
Equal Highs / Equal Lows
Two or more price points at the same level. Create visible liquidity pools that institutions specifically target. "EQH" (equal highs) = BSL pool. "EQL" (equal lows) = SSL pool. Almost always get swept before major reversals.
LIQUIDITY
FVG (Fair Value Gap)
Three consecutive candles where the wicks of candle 1 and 3 do not overlap. The "gap" between them is an imbalance — price moved too fast, leaving unfilled orders. Price tends to revisit FVGs. Bullish FVG = support. Bearish FVG = resistance.
PRICE LEVELS
IPDA (Interbank Price Delivery Algorithm)
ICT's conceptual framework for how price is algorithmically delivered between interbank price levels. Price is "programmed" to reach specific PDRA levels within defined time windows. Foundation of ICT time-price theory.
ICT THEORY
Kill Zone
High-probability time windows for institutional order flow: Asian (8PM-12AM EST), London Open (2-5AM EST), London/NY Overlap (8-10AM EST), NY Open (7-10AM EST), NY PM (1-3PM EST). Only trade during kill zones for highest win rate.
TIMING
Liquidity Pool
A concentration of resting stop-loss orders at a predictable price level. Every swing high has BSL above it; every swing low has SSL below it. Equal levels, previous session highs/lows, and round numbers are the densest pools.
LIQUIDITY
MSS (Market Structure Shift)
A confirmed change in market structure direction — typically a decisive CHOCH followed by a new Lower High (in a former uptrend). More definitive than CHOCH alone. Entry signal for counter-trend trades on lower timeframes.
STRUCTURE
OTE (Optimal Trade Entry)
The 62–79% Fibonacci retracement of a swing move — where ICT teaches that institutions re-enter in the direction of the trend after a pullback. Combines with FVG and OB presence for highest-probability entries.
ENTRY
PDRA Levels
Prior Day/Week/Month High, Low, Close, and Midpoint. These are the algorithmic reference points that price is programmed to trade to. PDH/PDL = prior day high/low. PWH/PWL = prior week high/low. PMH/PML = prior month. Essential for daily bias.
PRICE LEVELS
POI (Point of Interest)
Any price level where institutions previously transacted — Order Blocks, FVGs, breaker blocks, mitigation blocks, liquidity voids. The generic term for any level worth monitoring for potential entry. "I have a POI at X."
GENERAL
Premium Zone
The upper 50% of any range (above the 50% midpoint). Institutions sell in premium zones. Retail traders buying near premium are buying "expensive." Avoid long entries in premium; seek short entries.
PRICE LEVELS
SSL (Sell-Side Liquidity)
Resting sell stop orders below swing lows and equal lows. Retail shorts place stops here; bullish traders place stop-losses here. Institutions drive price down to sweep SSL, collect the liquidity, then reverse upward.
LIQUIDITY
SMT Divergence
Smart Money Tool divergence — two correlated assets making opposite structure at a key level. E.g., EURUSD makes a new low but GBPUSD doesn't = bullish SMT divergence. One of ICT's highest-confidence reversal signals.
ICT SIGNALS
Stop Hunt / Liquidity Grab
Institutional price manipulation to collect stop orders. Price spikes through a key level (swing high/low), triggers stop-losses (creating volume/liquidity for the institution), then immediately reverses. A planned hunting operation, not random noise.
LIQUIDITY

Wyckoff & AMT

AMT (Auction Market Theory)
The theoretical framework that markets operate as two-way auctions — continually searching for prices that facilitate trade. When price finds acceptance (value), it consolidates. When it finds rejection, it rotates to find new value. All of VP is built on AMT.
THEORY
Accumulation
Wyckoff Phase where institutions are quietly buying (or selling for distribution) at low prices over an extended period. Price moves sideways as the "Composite Man" absorbs retail supply. Cannot be identified until complete.
WYCKOFF
Composite Man
Wyckoff's conceptual entity representing the collective institutional force — all large operators acting as a single intelligent participant. Useful mental model for attributing market movements: "What is the Composite Man trying to do here?"
WYCKOFF
Initial Balance (IB)
AMT term: the price range established in the first hour of a trading session (typically first 2 TPO periods in Market Profile). The IB defines the session's "opening range." Breakouts above/below IB set directional expectations for the day.
AMT
LPS (Last Point of Support)
Wyckoff: the final pullback before a breakout from accumulation. Price makes a Higher Low — the last time supply overwhelms demand before the markup phase begins. The ideal entry point in Wyckoff accumulation setups.
WYCKOFF
SC (Selling Climax)
Wyckoff Phase A: a sudden, high-volume sell-off that signals the prior downtrend is exhausting. Bars are wide, volume is extreme, price closes off the lows. The first sign that selling pressure is being absorbed. Leads to Automatic Rally.
WYCKOFF
SOW (Sign of Weakness)
Wyckoff: price breaks below support with high volume and wide spread. Confirms distribution is complete and markdown is beginning. The distribution equivalent of SOS. Short entries on the next LPSY.
WYCKOFF
SOS (Sign of Strength)
Wyckoff: price breaks above resistance with high volume and wide spread on the upbar. Confirms accumulation is complete. The Composite Man "shows their hand." Long entries on the next pullback (Back to Ice) with smaller volume.
WYCKOFF
Spring
Wyckoff's most tradeable pattern: a false break below the support of an accumulation range. Shakes out weak holders, tests supply, and gives institutions a final low-price entry before markup. Confirmed when price snaps back above support with volume.
WYCKOFF
Upthrust
Wyckoff's distribution equivalent of the Spring — a false break above resistance of a distribution range. Traps longs before a markdown begins. Confirmed when price snaps back below resistance with increasing volume.

Cumulative Volume Delta — The Truth Detector

Why CVD is the single most powerful order flow indicator

CVD works on a simple but profound insight: price is not always telling the truth. Price can be pushed up by passive sellers hitting bids (bearish) even as the candle closes green. Price can fall on heavy aggressive buying (bullish) as institutions absorb sell pressure. CVD cuts through the deception.

📈 Bullish CVD Divergence

Price makes a Lower Low, but CVD makes a Higher Low. This means: despite price reaching a lower level, aggressive buyers are more active than sellers at those lows. Institutions are absorbing sell pressure — setup for reversal. Trade: long entry with order flow confirmation.

📉 Bearish CVD Divergence

Price makes a Higher High, but CVD makes a Lower High. This means: despite price reaching higher levels, aggressive sellers are increasing — institutions are distributing into the rally. Trade: short entry with order flow confirmation. One of the highest probability reversals in OFT.

The Wyckoff Spring — The Most Tradeable Pattern in Markets

From Fractal Flow's Ultimate Wyckoff Course

The Spring is Wyckoff's masterpiece — a false break below accumulated support that serves as the final shakeout before the markup phase. Understanding why it works reveals the genius of institutional thinking.

Why the Spring Exists:

  • Institutions need low prices to finish accumulating
  • Breaking support triggers retail stop-losses (creating sell orders = supply for institutions to buy)
  • Weak hands exit, strong hands absorb
  • After the spring: institutions have filled their full position at the lowest possible prices
  • Now they can push price up with minimal resistance

Spring Confirmation Checklist:

  • Price breaks below known support level
  • Volume is below average on the break (no real supply)
  • Candle closes back above support (wick rejection)
  • Volume increases on the recovery bar
  • CVD diverges bullishly during the break
  • Footprint shows absorption at the spring low
  • SOS follow-through within 3–5 bars

Entry: Close back above support. SL: 3 ticks below spring low. TP: Top of accumulation range.

QUESTION 01 / 40 — ORDER FLOW FOUNDATIONS
1. A candle closes green but Cumulative Volume Delta (CVD) is declining. What does this signal?
A) Strong bullish continuation — the move is confirmed
B) Bearish CVD divergence — sellers are absorbing the rally, reversal likely
C) Normal market behavior — CVD and price rarely agree
D) Low volume session — ignore CVD during these periods
✅ Answer: B — Bearish CVD divergence. Despite price rising, aggressive selling volume exceeds buying. Institutions are distributing into the rally. This is one of the highest-probability short setups when combined with a key resistance level.
QUESTION 02 / 40 — VOLUME PROFILE
2. Price is approaching a Low Volume Node (LVN) from below during a bullish trend. What do you expect?
A) Price will slow down and consolidate at the LVN
B) Price will reverse at the LVN (acts as resistance)
C) Price will accelerate rapidly through the LVN (no resistance)
D) LVNs have no predictive value in trending markets
✅ Answer: C — LVNs are thin areas with minimal historical trading activity. In trend direction, price moves quickly through LVNs as there's no significant resting supply/demand. Use LVNs as profit acceleration zones and target levels beyond them.
QUESTION 03 / 40 — MARKET STRUCTURE
3. In an established uptrend, price creates a CHOCH (Change of Character) on the 15-minute chart. What should you do?
A) Immediately go short — the trend has reversed
B) Close all longs immediately and wait
C) Check the Daily and 4H — if still bullish, treat the 15M CHOCH as a pullback opportunity, not a reversal signal
D) CHOCH on 15M is always irrelevant — only trade Daily structure
✅ Answer: C — A lower timeframe CHOCH is a warning signal, not confirmation of reversal. Multi-timeframe confluence is essential. A 15M CHOCH against a Daily bullish trend is often a liquidity grab/manipulation phase that sets up a higher-probability long entry.
QUESTION 04 / 40 — SMC / ICT
4. What is a "Breaker Block" and how does it differ from a standard Order Block?
A) A Breaker Block is just a stronger version of an Order Block
B) A Breaker Block is a failed Order Block that was violated — it now acts as a POI in the OPPOSITE direction
C) Breaker Blocks only exist on higher timeframes (Daily and above)
D) A Breaker Block occurs when price breaks through an OB with no reaction
✅ Answer: B — When a Bearish OB gets broken upward (price closes above it), the Bearish OB becomes a Bullish Breaker Block — it flips to act as support on retest. This represents a shift of institutional position, making Breaker Blocks some of the highest-probability POIs in ICT methodology.
QUESTION 05 / 40 — WYCKOFF
5. In Wyckoff accumulation, what makes the Spring superior to other entries?
A) The Spring provides the lowest risk entry because the SL is placed below a clear structural level
B) Springs always produce the largest profit moves
C) Springs are easy to identify in real-time
D) A and B are both correct
✅ Answer: A — The Spring's primary advantage is risk/reward. SL goes just below the spring low (a very specific, tight level), while the TP is the entire accumulation range and the markup phase above it. This creates exceptional R:R (often 4:1 to 8:1) with clear invalidation.
QUESTION 06 / 40 — BOOKMAP / TOOLS
6. On Bookmap, a large "bubble" appears at the ask side and repeatedly refreshes without being consumed. What is this?
A) An iceberg order — institutions hiding their true order size
B) A spoofing order — an order placed with no intent to fill
C) Normal retail limit order activity
D) Either A or B — only context can distinguish them
✅ Answer: D — Context distinguishes icebergs from spoofs. If the large bubble holds price AND absorbs aggressive buy orders (volume goes through it), it's an iceberg. If price approaches the bubble and it disappears (volume never executes there), it was spoofing. Both are taught in the Bookmap/MasiTrades video.

7. You have a $100,000 funded account with a 5% daily drawdown limit. How much should you risk per trade?
A) $5,000 per trade (5% — using your full daily limit)
B) $2,500 per trade (2.5% — half the daily limit, max 2 trades/day)
