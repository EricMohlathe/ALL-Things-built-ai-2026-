🌊 Order Flow Foundations

Understanding how institutional money moves price — the bedrock of professional trading

🎯 What is Order Flow?

Order flow is the raw data of buying and selling pressure at every price level in real time. Unlike price action alone, order flow reveals who is buying and selling, how much, and at which price. Institutions cannot hide their activity from order flow analysis — their large orders create footprints that trained traders can identify and exploit.

📖 Key Vocabulary

Bid: The highest price a buyer will pay.
Ask: The lowest price a seller will accept.
Spread: The difference between bid and ask.
Market Order: Executes immediately at best available price.
Limit Order: Resting order waiting at a specific price.
Aggressor: The side taking liquidity (market orders).
Passive: The side providing liquidity (limit orders).

🏗️ Market Microstructure

Price moves when aggressive market orders consume all available limit orders at a price level. When all bids at price X are consumed, price must drop to X-1 to find more. This "eating through" the order book is order flow in action. The Level 2 / DOM (Depth of Market) shows these resting orders before they're consumed.

The institutional map of price — understanding where the real money traded

🗺️ Volume Profile Structure

POC (Point of Control): The price level with the MOST volume traded — the "fairest price."

VAH (Value Area High): Upper boundary — 70% of all volume traded below this.

VAL (Value Area Low): Lower boundary — 70% of all volume traded above this.

Value Area: The range containing 70% of all traded volume. Price spends most time here.

🎯 HVN vs LVN — The Trading Edge

HVN (High Volume Node): Areas where price consolidated heavily. Acts as support/resistance magnet. Price often stalls and rotates at HVNs.

LVN (Low Volume Node): Thin areas where price moved fast with little trading. Acts as acceleration zone. Price tends to move rapidly through LVNs — your profit target placement zone.

🔺 BOS — Break of Structure

A Break of Structure confirms continuation. In an uptrend: price breaks above the previous Higher High = BOS = bullish continuation. A BOS on the same timeframe you're trading is the entry trigger.

🔄 CHOCH — Change of Character

A Change of Character signals potential reversal. In an uptrend: if price creates a Lower Low (breaks below a previous Higher Low), this is a CHOCH — first warning that the trend may be ending. Don't trade against higher timeframe until confirmed.

📐 Multi-Timeframe Alignment

Structure on the Daily gives macro bias. Structure on the 4H gives swing direction. Structure on the 1H/15M gives entry context. Only take trades where all 3 align — this is the "top-down analysis" framework used by all professional traders in this curriculum.

⚠️ Liquidity Hunting — The Bank's Game

Institutions require enormous liquidity to fill their orders. They cannot simply buy at market — they'd move price too much. Instead, they:

1. Identify where retail stop-losses cluster (below swing lows, above swing highs)
2. Drive price to those levels to trigger stops (creating liquidity)
3. Use the stop-triggered orders as their fill
4. Reverse price in their actual intended direction

This is the liquidity hunt / stop-hunt pattern — the most exploitable pattern in all markets.

✅ Trading With the Banks

1
Identify a pool of liquidity (equal highs/lows, previous session high/low)
2
Wait for price to sweep (break) the liquidity level with a wick or spike
3
Watch for immediate rejection — price closes back below the swept level
4
Look for LTF structure shift (CHOCH/MSS) confirming reversal
5
Enter on pullback to FVG/OB — SL above the wick, TP at opposite liquidity

Phase B: Building cause — price oscillates, institutions accumulate quietly. Signs of Strength (SOS) begin.

Phase E: Markup phase — price trends up as cause is distributed. Distribution beginning to appear at highs.

🗺️ Bookmap — Visual Order Book

What Bookmap Shows:
• Y-axis: Price levels
• X-axis: Time
• Color intensity: Volume of resting limit orders
• Yellow/bright = large orders (walls)
• Dark areas = thin liquidity

Key Patterns: Bid absorption (huge bids being maintained), ask stacking (sellers defending a level), iceberg orders (limit order hidden by size), spoofing (large orders that disappear before being hit).

📊 CVD — Cumulative Volume Delta

Formula: CVD = Σ(Aggressive Buys - Aggressive Sells)

Bullish Divergence: Price makes Lower Low but CVD makes Higher Low → buyers defending → reversal likely.

Bearish Divergence: Price makes Higher High but CVD makes Lower High → sellers absorbing → reversal likely.

Rule: Never fade a move confirmed by CVD. Only enter reversals when CVD diverges from price.

VWAP = Σ(Price × Volume) / Σ(Volume) VWAP Bands: VWAP ± (n × Standard Deviation of Price × Volume) Premium: Price above VWAP → institutions selling Discount: Price below VWAP → institutions buying Anchored VWAP (AVWAP): Anchor to key events (gap open, earnings, swing high/low)
