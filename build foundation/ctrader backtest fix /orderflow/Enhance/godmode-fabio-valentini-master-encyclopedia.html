02 — Theoretical Core

Auction Market Theory

The intellectual foundation for every trade Fabio takes.

AMT holds that markets are continuous auctions seeking the price where the maximum volume of transactions can occur. Price moves away from zones of acceptance (high volume) only when one side becomes more aggressive than the other can absorb.

AMT — Market State Cycle

Key AMT Principles

Balance (Equilibrium)

Price rotates around a fair value. Both buyers and sellers are satisfied. D-shaped volume profile develops. This is the default state — ~70% of time. Mean reversion model applies. Breakout attempts usually fail.

Imbalance (Directional)

One side (buyers or sellers) becomes more aggressive than the other can absorb. Price moves away from the old value area to discover new fair value. Elongated profile, LVNs crossed rapidly. Trend model applies.

AMT Core Insight
The market's primary job is to facilitate trade, not to trend. Trend is the exception, balance is the rule. Trading breakouts inside balance will produce a ~30% win rate on continuation. Trading reversion inside balance produces ~65–70% win rate. This single insight fundamentally changes your approach.

04 — Volume Profile

Volume Profile Mastery

Fabio's first teaching in the bootcamp — understanding where money actually traded, not just where price moved.

Volume Profile plots the amount of volume traded at each price level over a defined period, rather than over time. This reveals the auction's consensus — where both sides agreed to transact most — and the rejection zones — where one side pushed quickly through without transacting much.

Critical Profile Components

Volume Profile Anatomy — Balanced (D-Shape) vs Imbalanced (b-Shape)

Point of Control (POC)

The single price level with the most volume traded in the session or lookback period. This is the "fairest" price — where the most two-sided business occurred. Acts as a magnetic target when price moves away from it. ~70% probability of price returning to POC from any extreme.

Value Area (VA)

The price range containing 70% of the period's volume. Defined by VAH (Value Area High) and VAL (Value Area Low). Price inside VA = accepted/balanced. Price outside VA = potential imbalance or failed auction. Prior day VA is your most important reference each session.

High Volume Nodes (HVN)

Price levels with dense, clustered volume. Represent areas of acceptance and consensus. Price tends to slow, consolidate, or reverse at HVNs. Used as support/resistance levels in the trend model pullback entry.

Low Volume Nodes (LVN)

Thin volume areas where price moved rapidly without much transacting. Represent price rejection — neither side wanted to trade there. Critical concept: LVNs are your primary alert zones. When price approaches an LVN, expect either fast transit through OR a reaction. Set alerts at LVNs, never blind entries.

05 — Cumulative Delta

Cumulative Volume Delta (CVD)

The running score of the buyer vs seller battle — your primary confirmation tool.

CVD tracks the running total of aggressive buys minus aggressive sells. Aggressive buys = market orders lifting the ask. Aggressive sells = market orders hitting the bid. This creates a score: positive = net buyers in control, negative = net sellers.

CVD Patterns — What Each Signal Means

Bullish CVD Signals

  • CVD rising with price → trend confirmation, stay long
  • CVD rising while price flat → accumulation, expect breakout
  • CVD reverses from falling at support → absorption complete, long trigger
  • CVD spikes up aggressively → institutional buy, Model 1 entry

Bearish CVD Signals

  • CVD falling with price → trend confirmation, stay short
  • Price rising but CVD flat/falling → bearish divergence, exit long
  • CVD reverses at resistance → distribution, short trigger
  • CVD spikes down aggressively → institutional sell, Model 1 short
Critical CVD Rule
Move stop to break-even immediately if CVD shows strong opposing pressure after entry. CVD is your real-time thesis validator — if it breaks against you, your setup is invalidated. Don't wait for price to hit your stop.
06 — Footprint Charts

Footprint Chart Mastery

Reading every candle at the tick level — the unfair advantage available in DeepCharts.

A footprint chart displays the volume of aggressive buy and sell orders at every price tick inside each candle. The left number = sells at that price. The right number = buys at that price. This reveals the true internal battle hidden inside what looks like a simple bullish or bearish candle.

Key Footprint Patterns

Stacked Imbalances (Buy-Side)

When multiple consecutive price levels show significantly more buyers than sellers, cells are highlighted. Three or more stacked buy imbalances = institutional aggression. This is a high-conviction trend continuation signal in Model 1.

09 — Core Mechanic

Absorption Mechanics

The single most important concept in order flow trading — and the most misunderstood.

Absorption occurs when aggressive market orders are being absorbed by passive limit orders faster than they can move price. It's the invisible hand of an institutional player building a position by offering liquidity to retail traders trying to push price in one direction.

1
Aggressive Order Hits the Market Retail traders or algos send market orders in a direction (e.g., selling). These orders hit the bid aggressively. Normally, this depletes limit orders and price falls.
2
Limit Orders Replenished Faster An institutional player has a massive limit buy order at this level. They keep adding limit orders as fast as they're being consumed. Price barely moves despite heavy selling pressure.
3
What You See in Order Flow Large sell delta on the footprint (sellers active), but price is NOT moving down — it's stalling or micro-bouncing. CVD falling but price flat. This delta/price divergence = absorption signature.
4
Sellers Exhaust Their Aggression Eventually the aggressive sellers run out of conviction or capital. The limit buyer has now accumulated a large position. When aggressive selling stops, the accumulated long position drives price up sharply.
5
The Trade Entry When you see the aggression pivot (sellers giving up, CVD turning up, large buy prints appearing) — this is your entry trigger. Stop goes 1–2 ticks below the absorption low. Target: prior POC.
Absorption = High-Probability Entry
Absorption events at known volume profile levels (VAL, LVN below support, prior POC) combine two confluences — location and order flow mechanics. This is Fabio's highest-conviction setup. These entries are characterized by tight stops and large R:R because the institutional player's defense point is well-defined.
10 — Core Framework

The Triple-A Framework

Absorption → Accumulation → Aggression — the three-phase institutional trading pattern.

Triple-A Pattern — Price & Volume Behavior

Phase 1 — Absorption

Heavy one-sided order flow (usually selling) hits a level. Price barely moves. Institutional player is passively absorbing all aggressive orders. High volume, low range = absorption signature. This continues until the aggressor runs out.

Phase 2 — Accumulation

Volume contracts. Range tightens. Price compresses. The institutional player has finished building their position. Retail sees "nothing happening" and gets bored. This is the coil before the spring. LVN above/below signals the likely direction.

Phase 3 — Aggression

Institutional player activates. Large aggressive orders suddenly appear. Volume expands. CVD spikes. Price breaks cleanly through the accumulation zone. This is the entry moment — the print ≥30 contracts is your trigger.

12 — Execution Model

Model 1 — Trend Continuation

Out-of-balance market seeking new fair value. Ride the institutional aggression to the next profile level.

Activation Condition
Price has opened outside prior day's value area, OR has broken out of balance with strong directional CVD. Prior day closed near extreme. Multiple LVNs visible in the direction of the move. NY session preferred.

Long Setup — Step by Step

1
Mark the Impulse Leg

Identify the strong directional move that broke structure. Draw a volume profile from the start of this impulse to its peak. Find the LVN within the retracement zone — this is where you will set an alert.

2
Wait for Retracement to LVN/HVN

Do not enter on the initial impulse. Price will pull back. Allow it to come back to the volume profile level. The LVN or prior HVN support absorbs initial selling. Set the alert — never a blind limit order.

3
Confirm Absorption at Level

At your alert level, watch the footprint: sellers arriving but price not falling = buyers defending. CVD stalls or reverses. Deep Walls showing passive buy limit order support. This confirms institutional interest at this price.

4
Enter on Aggression Print

Wait for ≥30 NQ contract buy orders to appear — the institutional breakout. CVD spikes upward. Enter long. Place stop 1–2 ticks below the aggression candle low (NOT below the obvious swing low).

5
Target Prior POC — Full Exit

Target = prior day POC or the next identified balance level. Close 100% of the position there. 70% probability of reversal at POC. Do not hold for more. If structure is exceptional (rare trend day), trail stop only after POC is taken.

Short Setup

Mirror of long: Open below prior VAL, strong sell CVD, LVNs below as targets. Wait for bounce to HVN/LVN resistance. Confirm sell absorption (buyers trying but sellers absorbing). Enter on ≥30 contract sell print. Stop 1–2 ticks above aggression candle high. Target: prior POC below.

✅ Valid Model 1 Conditions

  • Clear gap open or strong directional drive
  • CVD trending from session open
  • Clean LVN visible as target
  • Retracement to identifiable profile level
  • ≥30 contract aggression trigger
  • R:R ≥ 1:3
  • NY session (preferred) or London first hour

❌ Model 1 Invalidation

  • Price reclaims back inside prior value area
  • CVD diverging against trade direction
  • Large opposing absorption at entry level
  • Setup breaks in first 5 min post-entry
  • Market compressing (no follow-through)
  • Pre-news blackout within 30 min
  • 3rd consecutive loss of the session
13 — Execution Model

Model 2 — Mean Reversion

Balanced market failing outside value — snap back to the POC. London session's primary opportunity.

Activation Condition
Price opened inside prior day's value area. Session developing as D-shape balanced profile. Price attempts to break out of balance but shows NO follow-through. CVD oscillating (not trending). London session or compressed market conditions.

Long (Failed Breakdown) Setup

1
Identify the Balance Zone

Use prior day profile VAH/VAL as balance boundaries. Mark the POC as the primary target. Confirm current session is developing inside this prior value area.

2
Watch for Failed Breakdown Below VAL

Price probes below the VAL. First probe is almost always a false break — do not enter on first move. Watch what happens: do sellers follow through with CVD expansion? If not → second probe coming.

3
Reclaim Inside Balance

Price comes back inside the VAL. Now it's confirmed: the breakdown failed. Wait for a pullback into the reclaim leg's LVN. Apply a volume profile on just the reclaim leg and find the LVN within it.

4
Enter at LVN on Aggression

At the reclaim leg LVN, watch for large buy prints (≥30 contracts) confirming institutional buying of the dip. CVD turns up. Enter long. Stop 1–2 ticks below the extreme low of the failed breakdown (not the obvious low — just beyond it).

5
Target the Session POC — Full Exit

The POC is your only target. This is where 70% of the session's volume transacted — it's the magnetic center. Exit 100% there. Do not stretch for the opposite extreme unless conditions are truly exceptional.

Model 2 Stop Placement Critical Note
Never place stops at the obvious swing low (the extreme). Place them 1–2 ticks beyond the LVN entry trigger candle. Why: when price accelerates through obvious stops, you get slipped at a far worse price. Placing stops at the technical invalidation level (aggression candle) gives you a tighter, better-executed stop.
14 — LVN Playbook

LVN Execution Playbook

Low Volume Nodes are the most precise entry zones in the entire system.

LVN Behaviour Rules

LVN as Reaction Zone

When price enters an LVN, two outcomes are equally possible: fast transit through (imbalanced day) or sharp reaction/reversal (balanced day). Never place a blind limit order at an LVN. Set an alert. Let price arrive. Then read the Order Flow to decide which outcome is occurring.

LVN as Speed Indicator

If price blows through the LVN without slowing → confirms imbalance/trend state. The LVN becomes a potential support zone (long re-entry on Model 1 pullback). If price stalls at LVN → check absorption → potential Model 2 entry if within balance context.

LVN Alert Protocol

  1. Mark all LVNs on prior day and current session profile each morning
  2. Set platform alerts 1–2 ticks before each LVN (not exactly at it)
  3. When alert fires, activate Order Flow observation mode
  4. Read footprint: aggressive prints or absorption?
  5. Aggressive + correct direction = enter. Absorption forming = wait for exhaustion, then enter reversion.
  6. No clear signal at LVN = skip (move to next level)

16 — Execution Detail

Stop Loss Placement Philosophy

The most counter-intuitive — and most profitable — aspect of Fabio's execution.

"Most traders put stops above the high. That's where everyone gets slipped. Smart traders exit 1–2 ticks before. Because when price accelerates, it's already too late." — Fabio Valentini, ChartFanatics Short

❌ The Obvious Stop (Retail Trap)

Placing stops directly above/below swing highs/lows or candle wicks. This is where algorithms hunt for liquidity. The stop is triggered, price reverses, and you're out at the worst price with maximum slippage. This is a predictable pattern that market makers exploit.

Stop = 1 tick above swing high → Gets hunted, fills poorly

✅ The Aggression-Based Stop

Place the stop 1–2 ticks beyond the aggression candle that triggered entry — not the prior swing high/low. The thesis is: if the institutional aggressor's candle is violated, the setup is invalidated. This gives you a tighter, better-placed, better-filled stop.

Stop = Aggression candle low - 2 ticks → Thesis-based, tight, clean fill

Stop Management Rules

  • Never widen the stop — widening means your thesis was wrong. Cut losses immediately.
  • Move to break-even when: CVD shows strong pressure in trade direction, OR price has moved 1:1 toward target
  • Defensive exit without stop: if CVD reverses aggressively against position, exit manually before stop is hit
  • Never average down — adding to a losing position is capital destruction
17 — Exit Rules

Exit & Target Discipline

The 70% rule and why partial exits destroy expectancy.

Fabio's exit rule is simple and non-negotiable: close 100% of the position at the first target. The reason is statistical:

Probability of reaching POC = ~70% | Probability of extending beyond POC = ~30%
Expected value of full exit at POC > Expected value of holding for extension

Holding for "more" means risking a confirmed winner for a 30% probability. Over 100 trades, this destroys expectancy. Fabio's model is built on high frequency + full exits at realistic targets, not lottery tickets.

22 — Mental Game

Trading Psychology

The rules Fabio lives by to maintain edge under pressure.

The 3-Loss Rule

After 3 consecutive stop-outs, stop trading for the session. Three losses in a row signal one of two things: (1) market conditions don't match the model today, or (2) you're making execution errors. Either way, more trades = more losses. Walk away. Review. Return tomorrow.

No Revenge Trading

The emotional drive to recover a loss immediately is the single most destructive force in trading. It leads to: oversized positions, trading unqualified setups, forcing entries, ignoring OF signals. Revenge trades statistically lose at a higher rate than normal trades.

No Directional Bias

Do not arrive at a session with a predetermined direction. Macro views, yesterday's trend, community sentiment = all noise. Read the current session's auction fresh. The market doesn't care about your opinion. Respond to what is happening, not what you think should happen.

Full Attention Required

This strategy is not passive. Active management is required: monitoring CVD, reading footprint, adjusting stops, recognizing when the thesis breaks. If you cannot give the session 100% attention — do not trade. Half-attention = execution errors.

Drawdown Acceptance

Multiple consecutive small stop-outs are expected and normal in this model. The strategy compensates with large winners and high frequency. Psychological preparation: 7 small losses in a row is entirely consistent with a 55% win rate system. Know your statistical expectation.

Capital Protection Priority

Fabio's repeated emphasis: protecting capital comes before making money. A trader who protects capital stays in the game long enough for the statistical edge to manifest. A trader who chases returns risks ruin. Stay small, stay consistent, let compounding work.

23 — Longevity

Edge Decay & Model Adaptation

Every trading edge eventually degrades. Understanding why — and how to prevent it.

Why Edges Decay

Regime Change

Volatility characteristics shift (e.g., 2023–2024 high-vol environment). Models calibrated to prior vol regimes produce different results. The mechanics don't change — the parameters do.

Crowding

When a strategy becomes widely known, competing traders enter at the same levels. The edge shrinks as more participants react to the same signals. AMT/OF is less susceptible to this due to its real-time nature.

Algorithm Changes

Major algo participants change their patterns, order size thresholds, and timing. If your Large Order filter was calibrated to old algo behavior, it may need recalibration.

Adaptation Protocol

Fabio's solution: understand the WHY deeply enough to adapt, not just the WHAT. If you know that LVN setups work because they represent volume rejection zones, you can adapt when the optimal LVN size or distance changes. If you only know "trade LVNs," you cannot adapt.

Practical adaptation steps:

  • Track profit factor by month — identify when it drops below 1.5
  • Segment win rates by market state — which model is underperforming?
  • Adjust order size filter — recalibrate institutional order threshold quarterly
  • Session performance analysis — is London or NY underperforming?
  • Volatility regime awareness — in high-vol environments, widen targets slightly
