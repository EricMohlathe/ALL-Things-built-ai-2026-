🎯 Core Thesis

Order flow exposes this battle in real time. Traders who read order flow are reading the actual cause of price movement, not just its effect.

1. Market Microstructure Fundamentals

📖 The Bid-Ask Engine

Bid: highest price a buyer will pay now.
Ask: lowest price a seller will accept now.
Spread: the gap between them — the market's transaction cost.

Every single market movement is caused by one side running out of orders at a price level. When all asks at 1.2000 are consumed, the next buy order must fill at 1.2001. This is price discovery.

⚡ Aggressive vs Passive Orders

Aggressive (Market) Orders: cross the spread immediately. Buyers pay the ask; sellers hit the bid. These consume liquidity and move price.

Passive (Limit) Orders: rest in the book, waiting. They provide liquidity. When passive orders are extremely large, they absorb aggressive flow and prevent price from moving — this is absorption.

2. Delta — The Directional Pressure Gauge

📊 Delta Definition

Delta = Aggressive Buys − Aggressive Sells within a candle or time period.

Positive delta → more buyers hit the ask than sellers hit the bid → net buying pressure.
Negative delta → more sellers hit the bid → net selling pressure.

Key insight: A green candle can have NEGATIVE delta (sellers were more aggressive even though price closed higher). This means passive buyers held price up against aggressive selling — very bullish absorption signal.

🟢

Positive Delta + Green Close

Strong bullish confirmation. Buyers aggressive and winning. Continue long or add to position.

🔴

Negative Delta + Red Close

Strong bearish confirmation. Sellers aggressive and winning. Continue short or add.

⚠️

Negative Delta + Green Close

Absorption! Passive buyers absorbing heavy selling. Bullish reversal signal. Watch for follow-through.

3. Cumulative Volume Delta (CVD)

📈 CVD — The Trend Indicator of Order Flow

CVD is the running total of delta across a session or chart period. Unlike individual candle delta, CVD reveals the macro buying/selling pressure trend. Rising CVD = net buying accumulation. Falling CVD = net selling accumulation.

4. The Footprint Chart

The footprint chart is the most powerful order flow visualization tool available to retail traders. Unlike standard candlesticks, each bar in a footprint chart shows the bid/ask volume at every single price tick within that bar.

🔍 Reading a Footprint Bar

Each row shows: [Sell Volume] × [Price] × [Buy Volume]

Example: 150 × 1.2003 × 340
→ 150 lots sold aggressively at 1.2003
→ 340 lots bought aggressively at 1.2003
→ Net delta at this price: +190 (strong buying)

The largest delta imbalances reveal where institutions transacted.

🎯 Footprint Patterns to Watch

Stacked Imbalances: 3+ consecutive price levels with same-side dominance. Extremely bullish or bearish — high momentum signal.

Unfinished Auction: Top of bar has 0 ask volume (buyers ran out) or bottom has 0 bid volume (sellers ran out). Price will return to complete the auction.

Absorption: Heavy volume on one side but price doesn't move. The other side is a wall.

Review Questions

Q1: A 15-minute candle closes bullish (green) but shows negative delta of -450. What is the most likely interpretation?
A) Error in the data — delta and candle direction should always agree
B) Bearish signal — sellers were more aggressive, reversal likely
C) Bullish absorption — passive buyers held price up against heavy selling; very bullish
D) The candle is meaningless and should be ignored
✅ C — This is classic absorption. Massive sell pressure (negative delta) couldn't push price down because large passive buyers were absorbing every sell order. Price closed green despite the selling. This is one of the most bullish signals in order flow — institutions buying everything the market throws at them.
Q2: CVD has been falling steadily for 3 hours while price has been rising. What trade do you consider?
A) Long — price is clearly bullish
B) Short — bearish CVD divergence signals sellers are absorbing the rally
C) No trade — conflicting signals cancel each other out
D) Wait for price to make a new high before deciding
✅ B — Bearish CVD divergence. Despite price rising, the NET aggression is with sellers (CVD falling). This means institutions are selling into the rally — distributing their long positions to retail buyers. The move is likely unsustainable. Look for a short at the next key resistance level with order flow confirmation.
